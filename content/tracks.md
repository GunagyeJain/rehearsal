# Tracks and scenarios

Five tracks. **The user never completes all of them** — the path draws 8–12 scenarios from across the library. The full library stays browsable and ungated in Explore.

Tracks marked **MVP** are fully built for the first release. The rest are schema-ready with content written later.

Turn counts and time limits vary per scenario; they are given below and are authoritative.

---

## Track 1 — Fundamentals · MVP

The baseline skills every other track assumes.

### 1.1 Self-introduction · difficulty 1
**Setup:** what you study or work in.
**Scene:** You have just sat down in an interview room. Two interviewers are opposite you. Nothing has been said yet.
**Turns:** 2 — (a) "Introduce yourself in under two minutes." (b) One follow-up picking a specific thing they mentioned.
**Times:** 120s, then 90s.
**Rubric focus:** structure — who, what, one concrete detail, clean close.
**Ends:** after the follow-up answer. Interviewer thanks them; screen moves to analysis.

### 1.2 Explain something you built · difficulty 2
**Setup:** name of the project.
**Scene:** Someone technical but outside your field has asked what you have been working on.
**Turns:** 2 — (a) "What is it and why did you build it?" (b) "What was the hardest part?"
**Times:** 120s, 90s.
**Rubric focus:** clarity without jargon — a listener outside the field could follow it.
**Ends:** after the second answer.

### 1.3 Answer "tell me about yourself" · difficulty 2
**Setup:** the role or programme being applied to.
**Scene:** First question of a formal interview for {{role}}.
**Turns:** 1.
**Times:** 120s.
**Rubric focus:** relevance — the answer is aimed at the stated role, not a life story.
**Ends:** immediately after the single answer.

### 1.4 Explain a decision you made · difficulty 3
**Setup:** none.
**Scene:** You are asked to justify a choice you made recently — a tool, an approach, a plan.
**Turns:** 2 — (a) the decision and the reasoning. (b) A challenge: "What would you have done if that had not worked?"
**Times:** 90s, 90s.
**Rubric focus:** reasoning is stated as a trade-off, not an assertion.
**Ends:** after the challenge answer.

### 1.5 Summarise under time pressure · difficulty 3
**Setup:** a topic to summarise.
**Scene:** You have 60 seconds. That is the whole brief.
**Turns:** 1, with a visible countdown that is part of the design, not a warning.
**Times:** 60s hard stop.
**Rubric focus:** finishing — the answer lands before the cut, with a real conclusion.
**Ends:** at the timer, or when they stop.

### 1.6 Handle a question you cannot answer · difficulty 4
**Setup:** field of study or work.
**Scene:** An interviewer asks something genuinely outside what you know.
**Turns:** 2 — (a) a hard question in the user's field. (b) "So what would you do to find out?"
**Times:** 60s, 60s.
**Rubric focus:** admits the gap without collapsing, then redirects to method.
**Ends:** after the second answer.

## Track 2 — Professional · MVP

Workplace speaking. The MVP's second full track because it matches the most common stated goal.

### 2.1 Standup update · difficulty 1
**Setup:** what you are currently working on.
**Scene:** Daily standup. Six people waiting. You are next.
**Turns:** 1.
**Times:** 60s, target 30–45s.
**Rubric focus:** covers done / next / blockers, and stops. Brevity is the criterion here — the duration window is tight and deliberately so.
**Ends:** immediately.

### 2.2 Interview: strengths and weaknesses · difficulty 2
**Setup:** the role.
**Scene:** Mid-interview for {{role}}.
**Turns:** 2 — (a) strengths, with evidence. (b) A real weakness and what you did about it.
**Times:** 90s, 90s.
**Rubric focus:** every claim is backed by a specific instance.
**Ends:** after the second answer.

### 2.3 Explain a delay to a manager · difficulty 3
**Setup:** what is late.
**Scene:** Your manager asks for a status update on {{thing}}, which is behind.
**Turns:** 2 — (a) the status. (b) "So when will it be done?"
**Times:** 90s, 60s.
**Rubric focus:** leads with the fact, not the excuse; ends with a commitment.
**Ends:** after the commitment.

### 2.4 Disagree with a senior person · difficulty 4
**Setup:** none.
**Scene:** A senior colleague has proposed an approach you think is wrong. The room is waiting.
**Turns:** 3 — (a) the proposal, stated by the AI. (b) The user objects. (c) Pushback: "We have always done it this way."
**Times:** 90s, 90s.
**Rubric focus:** disagreement is specific and unhedged, and stays on the idea rather than the person.
**Ends:** after the response to pushback.

### 2.5 Give difficult feedback · difficulty 4
**Setup:** none.
**Scene:** A teammate has missed two deadlines. You are speaking to them one to one.
**Turns:** 3 — (a) AI opens as the teammate, unaware. (b) User gives feedback. (c) Teammate becomes defensive.
**Times:** 90s each.
**Rubric focus:** names the behaviour and the impact concretely, and does not retreat when pushed.
**Ends:** after the response to the defensive turn.

### 2.6 Salary or offer conversation · difficulty 5
**Setup:** the role and a target figure.
**Scene:** The offer has come in below what you wanted. The recruiter is on the call.
**Turns:** 3 — (a) recruiter states the offer. (b) User responds. (c) "That is the top of our band."
**Times:** 90s each.
**Rubric focus:** names a number, gives a reason, and does not apologise for asking.
**Ends:** after the final response.

## Track 3 — Academic

### 3.1 Project viva · difficulty 3
**Setup:** the project topic (free text — this is the scenario where topic input matters most).
**Scene:** Two examiners. Your project is on the table. The first question is coming.
**Turns:** 3 — (a) "Explain what you built and why." (b) A technical question derived from their answer. (c) A challenge to a choice they mentioned.
**Times:** 120s, 90s, 90s.
**Rubric focus:** answers the question actually asked; defends choices with reasons.

### 3.2 Defend a design decision · difficulty 3
**Setup:** the decision.
**Turns:** 2. **Times:** 90s, 90s.
**Rubric focus:** trade-off stated explicitly, alternative acknowledged.

### 3.3 Present under a time limit · difficulty 3
**Setup:** topic and a 2-minute limit.
**Turns:** 1. **Times:** 120s hard stop.
**Rubric focus:** conclusion reached before the cut.

### 3.4 Explain your research to a non-expert · difficulty 4
**Turns:** 2. **Times:** 120s, 90s.
**Rubric focus:** zero unexplained jargon.

### 3.5 Answer a hostile examiner · difficulty 5
**Turns:** 3. **Times:** 90s each.
**Rubric focus:** stays composed, engages the substance, does not concede a correct point.

## Track 4 — Social

### 4.1 Join a conversation already in progress · difficulty 2
**Turns:** 2. **Times:** 45s, 60s.
**Rubric focus:** opens with a hook into what was being discussed, not a cold introduction.

### 4.2 Small talk with a stranger · difficulty 2
**Turns:** 3. **Times:** 45s each.
**Rubric focus:** asks as much as it answers; no dead ends.

### 4.3 Tell a short story well · difficulty 3
**Setup:** something that happened to you.
**Turns:** 1. **Times:** 120s.
**Rubric focus:** has a setup, a turn, and an ending.

### 4.4 End a conversation politely · difficulty 3
**Turns:** 2. **Times:** 45s each.
**Rubric focus:** closes cleanly without over-explaining.

### 4.5 Networking introduction · difficulty 3
**Turns:** 2. **Times:** 60s, 90s.
**Rubric focus:** memorable, specific, and hands the conversation back.

## Track 5 — Stage

### 5.1 Two-minute pitch · difficulty 4
**Setup:** what is being pitched.
**Turns:** 1. **Times:** 120s.
**Rubric focus:** problem, solution, why you, and an ask.

### 5.2 Handle Q&A after a talk · difficulty 4
**Turns:** 3 — three unrelated audience questions. **Times:** 60s each.
**Rubric focus:** each answer is self-contained and finishes.

### 5.3 Open a talk · difficulty 4
**Turns:** 1. **Times:** 60s.
**Rubric focus:** first fifteen seconds earn attention without a throat-clearing preamble.

### 5.4 Give a toast · difficulty 3
**Setup:** occasion and person.
**Turns:** 1. **Times:** 90s.
**Rubric focus:** specific to the person, lands an ending.

### 5.5 Deliver bad news to a group · difficulty 5
**Turns:** 3 — announcement, then two reactions from the room. **Times:** 90s each.
**Rubric focus:** states the news in the first sentence; does not bury it.

## Content rules

- Every scene description is second person, present tense, and under 40 words.
- Scenes describe the situation, never the user's emotional state. "Two interviewers are opposite you" — never "you feel nervous."
- Each scenario ends the moment its final turn is answered. No closing pleasantries beyond one short line from the AI.
